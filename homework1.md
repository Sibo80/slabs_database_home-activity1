Management Levels & Information flow

1. Operational Level
-Management Level:Operational
-Corresponding IS Type:Transaction Processing System(TPS)
-Data Flow & Purpose:This level processes daily, routine transactions like sales, payments, stock, and clocking in. The data is detailed, current, and high-volume and it supports decision-making by keeping accurate day-to-day records so the business runs smoothly without errors

2. Tactical Level
-Management Level:Tactical
-Corresponding IS Type:Management Information System(MIS) / Decision Support System(DSS)
-Data Flow & Purpose:This level takes the data from TPS and turns it into summaries, reports, and comparisons, like monthly sales reports or budget vs actual spending. The data is summarized and mostly short-term. It supports decision-making by helping middle managers monitor performance, solve problems, and plan for the next few months

3. Strategic Level
-Management Level:Strategic
-Corresponding IS Type:Executive Support System(ESS)
-Data Flow & Purpose:This level uses highly summarized data from inside and outside the organisation, like market trends, competitor information, and long-term sales trends. The data is summarized, future-focused, and often from external sources. It supports decision-making by helping top managers and executives set long-term goals, decide on new products, and plan the future of the organisation

Technical Architecture Definitions

1. IT Infrastructure Diagram
An IT Infrastructure Diagram shows the physical assets of the organisation and it also shows the actual hardware like servers, computers, printers, routers, switches, and cables, and where they are located. It also shows how these physical devices are connected to each other through the network so its main purpose is to show what physical equipment exists and how it is physically connected

2. IT Landscape Diagram
An IT Landscape Diagram shows asset types at a logical level, not physical devices. Instead of showing a specific server or cable, it shows the types of systems and applications used, like the database system, email system, and HR system, and how they relate to each other. It focuses on what functions the systems perform and how information flows between them and it helps to understand the overall picture of systems without getting lost in physical details

3. IT Architecture Diagram
An IT Architecture Diagram outlines the concepts, norms, and standards that guide how IT should be built and used. It shows the rules, principles, and standards for things like security, data naming, software choices, and network design. It also shows the concepts that must be followed so that all systems work together in the same way. Its main purpose is to make sure everyone builds and uses IT in a consistent, secure, and organised way
